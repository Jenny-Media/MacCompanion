The essentials.

No account with us.

You use your Mac’s existing account to connect. We do not run a Mac Companion login, session relay, or remote desktop server.

No ads or app analytics.

Mac Companion does not include advertising, tracking, or a developer-operated analytics service.

Local credentials.

Saved passwords and SSH private keys use this iPhone’s device-only Keychain. They are excluded from the app’s iCloud sync.

Sync is your choice.

Syncing saved Macs and connection settings is off by default. You must explicitly enable it.

This policy covers the current direct iOS client and this product website. Jenny Media LLC develops Mac Companion. We do not sell personal information or use your desktop content for advertising.

Remote sessions go to your Mac.

Desktop and Trackpad & Keyboard connect to built-in macOS Screen Sharing through an SSH connection to the same Mac. Screen pixels, cursor information, and the input you send are processed on your Mac and iPhone. They are not sent through Jenny Media servers. The app does not automatically upload session recordings or keystrokes to us.

Desktop and input traffic use SSH encryption. The app verifies the Mac’s SSH server key before sending your login. Screen Sharing then runs inside that SSH connection, without a public relay. Use a trusted local network, or an active private VPN such as Tailscale on both devices. Do not expose Screen Sharing directly to the internet.

Terminal connects directly to the SSH server you choose. SSH encrypts its connection, and the app asks you to verify the server key before authentication. A changed server key stops login until you independently verify the Mac and explicitly update its saved trust. Your commands and their output are processed by that server and your iPhone; what the server records depends on its configuration.

A private VPN, your network provider, and the remote Mac are separate services or devices under your control. Their privacy and security practices also apply. Mac Companion does not provide a VPN or public relay.

What is stored on your iPhone.

  • Saved Macs and preferences: Mac names, addresses, ports, display selections, mouse settings, and app preferences are stored locally so you can reconnect and restore your view.
  • Saved logins: If you choose to remember a login, its username and password are stored in the device-only Keychain. Desktop and Terminal have separate saved logins for each Mac entry.
  • SSH keys and server trust: Named SSH private keys, their selections, and accepted server keys are stored in the device-only Keychain. Removing a Mac does not delete a reusable key from your key library.
  • Custom actions and snippets: Saved text and custom Terminal or remote actions remain local. Sensitive saved text uses protected Keychain storage.
  • Local diagnostics: The app may keep a bounded latest diagnostic snapshot of numeric connection counters and display geometry in its cache. It excludes addresses, Mac names, credentials, screen pixels, and typed content. It is not automatically uploaded to us.

Key import and export are actions you initiate. Exporting a private key requires device-owner authentication and a passphrase, and produces an encrypted OpenSSH file. Once you save or share an exported file, the destination you choose is responsible for it. Installing a key sends only its public key to your selected Mac account.

The optional app unlock uses Apple’s Face ID or device-passcode authentication. Mac Companion receives the authentication result, not your face scan or biometric template. This setting protects access within the app; SSH protects session transport, and your Mac still verifies its account login.

If enabled, Live Activities can show your saved Mac name and session state on Dynamic Island and the Lock Screen. Choose a non-sensitive Mac name or turn Session Status off in App Settings if you prefer not to show it there.

Optional iCloud sync.

Sync Saved Macs with iCloud is off by default. Enabling it submits the following information to iCloud Keychain for devices using your Apple Account:

  • Saved Mac identifiers and names.
  • Ordered local and private VPN addresses, plus Desktop and SSH ports.
  • Display choice, pointer/trackpad mode and speed, fullscreen controls, and Follow Cursor settings.

Passwords, SSH private keys, trusted server keys, saved text, and custom actions are not included. App appearance, app-unlock settings, and the choice to enable sync remain local.

These synced details can reveal your machines and network. Their privacy relies on Apple’s iCloud Keychain security, your Apple Account, and your trusted devices. Mac Companion adds no separate cloud password. Review Apple’s iCloud Keychain privacy information before enabling sync.

Turning sync off stops the app’s syncing, but does not remove existing cloud copies. Remove iCloud Copies is a separate confirmed action. Turn sync off on your other devices first so they do not upload the saved Macs again. Apple controls when changes and removal reach other devices.

Apple purchases and beta testing.

Apple handles App Store and TestFlight distribution, purchases, and payment information. Mac Companion uses StoreKit transaction information to check Lifetime Pro or the optional 14-day trial. We do not receive your payment-card details. Trial and purchase records are used to determine access, restore purchases, and enforce the original trial end date.

Apple may provide developers with app distribution information, crash reports, and feedback under your Apple settings and TestFlight choices. Feedback and screenshots you deliberately submit through TestFlight can reach us. Please remove passwords, keys, and sensitive screen content before submitting them.

Apple’s services are governed by Apple’s privacy policy. Your device backups and Apple Account settings may also affect app data; the app’s own sync exclusions do not describe every backup service you may choose.

Your choices and deletion.

  • Forget a login: Open that Mac’s settings and remove the saved Desktop or Terminal login. The other login is kept.
  • Remove a Mac: Remove its saved entry and logins from My Macs. Reusable keys remain in SSH Keys until you delete them there.
  • Delete an SSH key: Use the SSH Keys library. Deleting the local key does not remove a public key previously installed in a Mac’s authorized_keys.
  • Remove cloud copies: Turn off sync on other devices, then use App Settings → Remove iCloud Copies. Local Macs and logins are kept.
  • End a session: Leave the viewer through its session exit action, or use End in the expanded Live Activity.

Stored settings and secrets are retained until you remove or replace them. Do not rely on uninstalling alone to clear Keychain items; remove saved logins and keys in the app first. Files you exported, data on your Mac, Apple transaction records, and messages you sent to support must be managed separately.

Support messages and this website.

If you contact us, we receive your email address, message, and any attachments you choose to send. We use them to answer and investigate your request. Do not send passwords, private keys, or unredacted desktop content. Public GitHub issues are visible to others.

Support messages are kept as needed to resolve the request and maintain relevant correspondence. You can ask us to delete personal information from a support exchange, subject to records we must retain for legitimate legal or security needs.

This website includes no advertising or analytics scripts. It stores only your selected light, dark, or system appearance in your browser’s local storage. Hosting and email providers may process ordinary request or delivery information, such as IP addresses and browser details, to operate and protect their services. Links to Apple, GitHub, Jenny Media, or other websites lead to services with their own policies.
