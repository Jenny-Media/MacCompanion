Connect your Mac.

For Desktop or Trackpad & Keyboard, use your Mac’s built-in Screen Sharing. You do not need a Mac Companion app or helper on the Mac.

  1. On your Mac, open System Settings → General → Sharing.
  2. Open Screen Sharing settings, enable it, and allow the Mac account you want to use. If Remote Management is enabled instead, review Apple’s Screen Sharing instructions; macOS does not enable both at once.
  3. Put your iPhone and Mac on the same trusted network, or connect both to your private VPN.
  4. In Mac Companion, add a Mac name and its IP address or hostname. Use the address itself, without vnc:// or https://.
  5. Open the saved Mac and enter the Mac account’s username and password. This is not a separate Mac Companion account. Remembering the login on this iPhone is optional.

For Terminal, also enable Remote Login in General → Sharing and allow your account. See Apple’s Remote Login guide.

Connection or login fails

Check that the Mac is awake, the address is current, and Screen Sharing is enabled for the account you entered. In iPhone Settings, allow Mac Companion’s Local Network access when connecting locally. Check your Mac’s firewall and network restrictions.

Use the Mac account’s short username if its full name does not work. Desktop and Terminal keep separate credentials; a successful SSH login does not mean Screen Sharing is enabled. If a saved login is wrong, forget it in that Mac’s settings and try again.

The desktop is blank or stops updating

Wait briefly for the first image, then end the session and reconnect. Confirm the Mac is awake and the network still works. If it repeats, note whether it happened after a display change, Mac sleep, a Space change, or returning from the background, and send that sequence with the exact error.

The app is in private beta. We are still testing compatibility and recovery across Mac and network configurations.

Local network and Tailscale.

Desktop traffic is not encrypted by the app. Use a trusted local network or a private VPN. Do not forward Screen Sharing’s port to the public internet.

How do I connect away from home?

Install and enable Tailscale on both your Mac and iPhone, using a network where the iPhone is allowed to reach the Mac. Add the Mac’s Tailscale IPv4 or IPv6 address to its saved entry. Check that access rules allow the connection and that Screen Sharing or Remote Login is running on the Mac.

Mac Companion supports local and private VPN routes. It does not provide its own VPN, public relay, or router setup. See Tailscale’s installation guide for its setup.

Can one Mac have multiple addresses?

Yes. Open that Mac’s settings → Connection Addresses. Add your local and Tailscale addresses, then use Reorder to set their priority. The app tries addresses in order until a TCP connection succeeds; it does not keep trying other addresses after authentication begins.

Editing addresses or ports keeps that saved Mac’s login. Two saved entries may share an address and still keep separate credentials.

Can I change the ports?

Yes. Open Advanced in the Mac’s settings. Screen Sharing normally uses port 5900; SSH normally uses 22. Leave a field empty to use its default. Enter a port from 1 to 65535 for a custom service configuration.

Tailscale IPv4 fails but IPv6 works

You can save both addresses and put the working one first. Check that Tailscale is active on both devices and the Mac is reachable under your network’s access rules. If only one route works, include the address type and exact error in your report; do not include your login or private network details unless needed.

Displays and everyday controls.

How do I select an individual display?

Open the floating controls menu → View & Display → Choose Display. You can select an individual display when macOS supplies supported display-layout information. Otherwise, All Displays remains available. The app restores a saved choice only if it is valid for the current layout.

Selection changes your local viewport rather than creating a new connection. App/window selection and exact window smart zoom are not included in the direct client.

Pointer or Trackpad: which should I use?

Pointer puts the Mac cursor where you touch; tap to click. Trackpad moves the cursor relative to your finger, like a laptop trackpad. Adjust trackpad speed in Keyboard & Input → Input & Quick Actions. Hold and move to drag.

Use Trackpad & Keyboard from the Mac’s menu if you want to control a Mac connected to a TV without viewing the desktop on your iPhone.

Zoom, Follow Cursor, and the keyboard

Pinch to zoom and pan to move around. A two-finger double tap toggles between a closer view and fit. In zoomed Trackpad mode, Follow Cursor keeps your iPhone-driven cursor in view near the edges; it can be turned off in Input & Quick Actions.

Opening the software keyboard reduces the desktop viewport. Closing it restores your previous view where the display layout remains valid. The keyboard button changes to indicate that it will dismiss the keyboard.

How do modifiers and quick actions work?

Tap Command, Option, Control, or Shift for a one-shot modifier; it releases after the next key. Hold to lock a modifier, then tap it again to release. Escape, Tab, and extra navigation keys are also available.

Tap the floating controls button to open the menu, or hold it, slide to an action, and release to select. You can cancel by moving away. Pro adds custom shortcuts and saved-text actions.

Can I hide controls or end a background session?

Use fullscreen controls in View & Display to hide the keyboard and modifier bar. The floating controls button remains available so you can bring controls back.

Optional Session Status appears on Dynamic Island and the Lock Screen. Expand it to Resume or End the matching session. This is not a guarantee of an indefinitely active background connection; iOS may suspend the app, and recovery checks the connection when you return.

Terminal and SSH keys.

How do I start a Terminal session?

Enable Remote Login for your account on the Mac. In Mac Companion, open that Mac’s menu and choose Terminal. Verify the SSH server fingerprint through a trusted source before approving it, then use a password or selected SSH key.

Terminal is an SSH connection, independent of Screen Sharing. You can use Terminal without opening Desktop.

Where do I create, import, or export SSH keys?

Open App Settings → SSH Keys. Create an Ed25519 key, import a supported OpenSSH key, rename it, or export its public key. In a Mac’s settings, select a named key for that Mac account.

Private-key export requires fresh device-owner authentication and a nonempty passphrase. Keep the encrypted export in a secure place. Current key support is Ed25519; RSA, ECDSA, and hardware-backed keys are not supported.

The free tier includes one active SSH key and key import/export. Pro adds multiple named keys. You can reuse a key or choose a different one for each Mac.

What does Install Key on This Mac do?

This Pro action is in the individual Mac’s settings. Choose a key and authenticate with an existing password or working key. The app appends its public key to that account’s authorized_keys, preserves other entries, and verifies a fresh key-only login before selecting it.

No Mac app or helper is installed. If setup times out or is canceled, the public key may already be present; the app does not retry the command automatically.

SSH warns that the server key changed

Stop and verify the new fingerprint through a trusted channel. A legitimate reinstall or SSH configuration change can replace a server key, but a mismatch can also mean you reached a different server. Only forget the saved server key in that Mac’s settings after you have verified the change.

How do I send Control-C or use the number row?

The Terminal keyboard includes an always-visible number row. Ctrl and Alt apply once when tapped, or stay locked when held. More contains Shift, navigation/function keys, and common Ctrl-C/D/Z shortcuts. Pro adds a custom key row and saved snippets.

Choosing a snippet that contains a newline can execute a command on your Mac. Review saved text before sending it.

Saved Macs and privacy settings.

Will editing an address erase my login?

No. Address and port edits preserve the login for that saved Mac. Saved credentials belong to the Mac entry, so two entries with the same address can have different logins. Forget a saved Desktop or Terminal login separately in the Mac’s settings if you want to replace it.

How do I enable Face ID or change the theme?

App Settings offers optional app unlock using Face ID or your device passcode. It is separate from your Mac’s login. Under Appearance, choose System, Light, or Dark. Terminal colors have their own Follow App, Light, or Dark choice.

What does iCloud sync include, and how do I stop it?

Sync Saved Macs with iCloud is off by default. It syncs saved Mac names, addresses, ports, and display/input settings through iCloud Keychain. Passwords, private keys, server trust, saved text, and custom actions stay on this device.

Its protection relies on your Apple Account, trusted devices, and iCloud Keychain. Turning sync off does not erase cloud copies. To remove those, turn off sync on other devices and use Remove iCloud Copies. See the full iCloud privacy explanation.

Free, Lifetime Pro, and the trial.

What stays free?

One saved Mac, one active SSH key, Desktop, Trackpad & Keyboard, basic SSH Terminal, standard keys and the number row, multiple addresses and ports, display selection and zoom. Security, accessibility, and key import/export remain free.

How does the 14-day trial work?

You start it explicitly from App Settings → Pro. It unlocks Pro features for 14 days from the original verified enrollment. It does not renew or charge automatically. Restoring or reinstalling does not restart it; basic features remain available after it ends.

What does Lifetime Pro add?

Multiple saved Macs and named SSH keys, Install Key on This Mac, custom remote quick actions, custom Terminal keys, and saved text snippets. The planned U.S. price is $9.99 once; your local App Store price is shown before purchase.

How do I restore Pro or test purchases?

In App Settings → Pro, choose Restore Purchases using the Apple Account associated with the purchase or trial. Existing extra Macs and keys remain editable and exportable if your entitlement changes; an active connection is not ended by a purchase-state change.

In TestFlight, purchase tests use Apple’s sandbox and do not charge your account. The app’s products are not yet publicly available.

Availability and compatibility.

Mac Companion is currently in private internal TestFlight testing. There is no public download or open beta link yet. Invited testers can accept their email invitation and install the app through TestFlight.

The iPhone app requires iOS 26 or later. It connects to built-in macOS Screen Sharing and, optionally, Remote Login. Current physical testing has used macOS 27; compatibility with other Mac versions remains under testing.

Audio streaming, file transfer, a public relay, individual app/window capture, and exact window fitting are not part of the current direct client.

Mac Companion uses open-source components. See license and dependency credits. For a reproducible bug, you can open a public issue; remove personal or network details from screenshots first.
